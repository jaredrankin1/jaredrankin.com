        Case study · ReUseBricks

        BrickStash: building trust into LEGO resale, from intake to shipping

        A highly rated seller sold us expensive convention-exclusive minifigures that turned out to be fake. Good reviews couldn't guarantee authenticity, so the shop needed its own checks, and so did its own listings. I designed and built a grading, inventory and listing tool that adds quality control at every step, from the moment a fig arrives to the moment it ships.

        The problem

        The shop sold only on BrickLink, and nothing about a fig's identity or condition was standardised. The counterfeit purchase made the gap obvious: reviews measured the seller, not the item. That became the design question. How do you make every single fig verifiable, both coming in and going out?

        • Misidentified figs. Figs were often misidentified at intake, and picking an order for one could take hours or fail.
        • Undisclosed wear. Listings defaulted to new, and condition issues surfaced at packing or as refunds.
        • Split listings. BrickLink allows one remark per lot, so a fig stored in two places appeared as two listings.
        • Slow picking. Without reliable locations, finding a single fig could take hours.
        • Manual sync. Once Shopify was added, the same fig could sell on both channels in one day, and reconciling stock by hand took hours.
        • Flat-rate shipping. Every order paid the same high rate, regardless of size.

        Approach

        I applied the Toyota Production System, 5S and Lean to a small resale floor. The rule: catch defects at intake, where fixing them is cheap, rather than at packing or after a refund.

        1. One grading standard. Every fig is checked part by part and gets a grade, and disclosed cracks cap it.
        2. One record per physical copy. Each copy has its own serial, condition, location and cost, so the system mirrors the shelf.
        3. One source of truth for stock. Sales channels read from BrickStash instead of each keeping their own count.
        4. Visual, walkable locations. Pick lists say where to walk, building on the 5S warehouse layout.

        It builds on the floor work around it: time-on-task tracking, visual SOPs for product photography, and NIOSH-informed ergonomic changes at the packing station.

        Alongside the software, I fixed the physical side: switching figs from flat-rate boxes to bubble mailers cut shipping cost for most minifig orders by about 59%.

        Design decisions

        A grading scale, designed twice. Version one borrowed the diamond 4 Cs, a model collectors already trust, and adapted it into five Cs: Compression, Color, Cracking, Clarity and Chafing. It was memorable but harder to read at a glance, so I rewrote it in plain words. The shipped scale scores four criteria from 1 to 10, and cracks moved out of the score into a separate disclosure.

        A grade only builds trust if graders and buyers read it the same way. Plain labels made it faster to learn and harder to misread, and separating cracks keeps the most serious defect from being averaged away.

        AI suggests, an expert decides. An AI vision model proposes the fig's identity and flags wear, and I review every photograph before a grade is issued. This follows ASA guidance that AI can support the research and analysis behind an appraisal, while the final opinion must come from a human appraiser. The model handles speed and recall; a person stays accountable for the call. That matters because each grade carries a certificate number. I'm a member of the American Society of Appraisers and a candidate for accreditation, with coursework in USPAP and the ASA personal property series, and I perform appraisals for insurance, estates, sales and divorce, so a grade has to hold up to scrutiny.

        Designing for uncertainty. The scan flags marks it isn't sure about ("Check this one, it may be glare or a mould line"), and the seller keeps or dismisses each one while the grade updates live. Better capture guidance up front means fewer false marks to dismiss.

        From grade to price. The suggested price is the market index price times a grade multiplier, and a perfect grade is floored at the six-month sold average for new. Honest grading never prices below what the market supports.

        Outcomes

        • Pick-and-pack time fell from 14 minutes to 4 on average, a 71% cut. This reflects the software and the floor changes together.
        • Shipping cost per minifig order fell about 59% after moving from flat-rate boxes to bubble mailers.
        • A second sales channel became workable. With one synced stock count, Shopify sessions reached 32,804 from January 1 to October 30, 2026, up 757% on the same period in 2025. The rebrand and social media growth contributed too.
